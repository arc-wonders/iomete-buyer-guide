## Key Question 1: Company facts (founding, founders, HQ, headcount, funding, YC, hires)

### Takeaway
IOMETE is a seed-stage, YC W22 company founded in 2021 by Vusal Dadalov (Azerbaijani ex-Uber engineer, CEO) and Piet Jan de Bruin (Dutch, COO), registered in Mountain View CA with a remote team concentrated in Amsterdam and Azerbaijan; total disclosed funding is small (sources range from $0.5M to ~$2.1M) with Y Combinator, Caucasus Ventures, Thunder Future, Eastlink Capital and Velar Capital listed as investors.

## Key Question 3: Target industries, regions and regulatory drivers

### Takeaway
IOMETE targets regulated, sovereignty-sensitive buyers (banks and central banks, public sector, telecom, healthcare) primarily in the EU plus Central Asia, the Caucasus, the Middle East and emerging Asia, and anchors its pitch in DORA, NIS2, GDPR, the EU AI Act, the US CLOUD Act and national sovereignty mandates.

### Cited Findings
- Target sectors: "banks, public institutions, telecom companies, and other regulated organizations"; buyers increasingly weigh data location, operator identity and reliance on outside providers — [Webrazzi](https://webrazzi.com/2026/09/11/kurumlarin-veriyi-kendi-altyapilarinda-tutmasina-odaklanan-girisim-iomete/)
- LinkedIn description: target "regulated and air-gapped environments"; marketed for "replacing Hadoop and legacy warehouses"; emphasis on sovereignty, predictable vCPU licensing, open standards, Kubernetes-native — [LinkedIn: IOMETE](https://www.linkedin.com/company/iomete/)
- Regions named for sovereignty mandates: "Central Asia, the Caucasus, the Middle East, and parts of Africa" plus EU — [Data lakehouse for banks](https://iomete.com/resources/blog/data-lakehouse-for-banks)
- Regulations cited with articles: DORA (in force 17 Jan 2025) Art. 8, 9, 17–19, 25, 28–30; EU AI Act Art. 10, 12, 13 (high-risk obligations now 2 Dec 2027 per Digital Omnibus) — [DORA & private cloud, Jun 2026](https://iomete.com/resources/blog/data-sovereignty-regulated-finance-dora-private-cloud)
- Also cited: NIS2 (essential entities, healthcare), GDPR row-level subject rights, TCFD disclosures, central bank directives — [Data lakehouse for banks](https://iomete.com/resources/blog/data-lakehouse-for-banks); DORA Art. 28, NIS2, GDPR, US CLOUD Act exposure of US-incorporated vendors — [Databricks alternatives, Mar 2026](https://iomete.com/resources/blog/databricks-alternatives)
- CLOUD Act: "Encryption Does Not Guarantee Data Sovereignty" (Mar 3, 2026) — [CLOUD Act blog](https://iomete.com/resources/blog/cloud-act-reality-check); EU Data Act referenced for switching/egress rights — [Public sector blueprint, Aug 2026](https://iomete.com/resources/blog/public-sector-data-sovereignty-platform)
- Residency vs sovereignty distinction post (Feb 24, 2026) — [Data Residency vs Data Sovereignty](https://iomete.com/resources/blog/data-residency-vs-data-sovereignty)
- Sovereignty definition: three properties, "modern architecture, flexible deployment, and real control"; without all three a platform is "hosted, not sovereign"; "no vendor-operated data plane in the path of customer data"; "open formats mean no exit cost" — [What is a sovereign data platform, May 2026](https://iomete.com/resources/blog/what-is-a-sovereign-data-platform)
- Homepage: Databricks and Snowflake "run on US public clouds only"; "Save 2-5x over SaaS/cloud"; SaaS is "suboptimal for petabyte-scale deployments" — [Homepage](https://iomete.com/)
- Public sector agency archetypes: tax authority, health ministry, national statistics office; air-gapped operation (licensing, auth, scheduling, upgrades offline); three topologies: single-cluster, multi-data-centre, disconnected — [Public sector blueprint](https://iomete.com/resources/blog/public-sector-data-sovereignty-platform)
- Healthcare: HIPAA badge and "patient data" in 2023 case study — [Homepage](https://iomete.com/); [On-premise case study](https://iomete.com/resources/blog/on-premise-case-study)
- Deployment flexibility: on-prem, AWS, Azure, GCP, hybrid, regional sovereign clouds — [Deployment](https://iomete.com/product/deployment)

### Inferences
- Primary ICP: a bank, central bank or ministry with a 12–20-year-old Oracle warehouse and/or Hadoop estate, a Kubernetes team (or willingness to build one), petabyte-scale ambitions and a legal reason not to use US hyperscaler SaaS.
- Defense and energy are not explicitly named in any source; "air-gapped" messaging implies defense-adjacent appeal but is unproven.
- Telecom is named only in the Webrazzi article (and the founder's Azercell background); no telecom use case content found.

### Gaps
- No US-market regulatory messaging (e.g., FedRAMP, state privacy laws) found; US positioning appears limited to Mountain View address and SOC 2/HIPAA.
- No explicit Middle East customer or partner found despite region being cited.
