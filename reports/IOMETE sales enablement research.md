# Ground IOMETE's Buyer Tool in Cited Facts

Build the security questionnaire helper first and the business-case builder second, and build both on one shared evidence store that tags every statement as documented, inferred or unknown; the other four candidate frictions either duplicate $69–99/user commercial tools, depend on data sources this research could not even reach (LinkedIn returned HTTP 999, G2 and Capterra 403), or require product access a hackathon team will not get. IOMETE is a seed-stage, Y Combinator W22 vendor (roughly 20–45 people, under $3M in disclosed funding, with sources disagreeing on every figure) selling **proprietary, self-hosted lakehouse software built on Apache Spark and Apache Iceberg** that installs as a Helm chart into the customer's own Kubernetes cluster, is licensed at **$500 per vCPU per year with a $100,000 annual minimum**, and is sold through a demo-led, form-gated field motion to banks, central banks and ministries concentrated in Azerbaijan and Uzbekistan, plus one described Dell Technologies deployment. Its documentation now lives at **https://iomete.com/resources/** (docs.iomete.com no longer resolves), is open source in the **iomete/iom-docs** GitHub repository, exposes a **482-URL sitemap** and an **llms.txt**, and is admirably explicit about limits in its release notes; the marketing layer above it, however, contradicts itself on cost savings (2–5x, 2–3x, 30–60%, 40–60%, and a worked example showing 1%), on data lineage, on the dbt adapter, on the control-plane architecture and on edition names. Security is the sharpest asymmetry: identity, access policy, masking, audit and secrets are thoroughly documented, while the vendor's corporate posture rests on a single **SOC 2 Type I report from December 2022**, with Type II and ISO 27001 still "readiness" projects in a September 2026 job posting and penetration testing, SBOM, breach notification and telemetry mechanics unpublished. That asymmetry, together with sales benchmarks showing the security/legal/procurement "paper process" adds 30–90 days and that discussing ROI late cuts win rates 64%, is why the two recommended tools score about 8.2/10 on the track's 35/25/25/15 weighting versus 4–7 for the rest. Each must refuse to assert roughly two dozen things IOMETE's own site implies but does not prove, and each can be proven with a zero-fabrication test on the known unknowns and by reproducing IOMETE's own published arithmetic.

## IOMETE is proprietary software on open engines, running only inside the buyer's Kubernetes

IOMETE's current self-description is "The sovereign data platform" with the subhead "Your data, your future" and the definition "A sovereign data lakehouse, self-hosted in your trusted environment" ([IOMETE homepage](https://iomete.com/)); its llms.txt calls it "a Kubernetes-native, self-hosted sovereign data platform and data lakehouse" built on Spark and Iceberg that runs entirely within the customer's environment and is deployed with Helm charts and Terraform ([iomete.com/llms.txt](https://iomete.com/llms.txt)). The vocabulary has shifted three times: the 2022 Data Engineering Podcast episode framed it as "an open and affordable lakehouse platform" ([Data Engineering Podcast E332](https://www.dataengineeringpodcast.com/episodepage/making-the-open-data-lakehouse-affordable-without-the-overhead-at-iomete)), a 2022-era Capterra listing described "a fully-managed, complete, open source, lakehouse platform for AWS users" ([Capterra NZ](https://www.capterra.co.nz/software/1034093/iomete)), the GitHub organisation still says "The Self-Hosted Data Lakehouse Platform" ([github.com/iomete](https://github.com/iomete)), and 2026 copy says "sovereign." A tool should treat "self-hosted" and "sovereign" as synonyms in IOMETE's mouth and treat anything describing a managed AWS service as stale.

The word "open" needs precision. The platform itself is closed source: the Software License Agreement (last updated March 16, 2026) grants a "limited, non-transferable, non-sublicensable, non-exclusive" licence for internal business purposes, prohibits reverse engineering, and states the software is "self-hosted enterprise software" and "not provided as a hosted or software-as-a-service (SaaS) offering" ([Software License Agreement](https://iomete.com/policies/software-license-agreement)). The 24 public repositories are docs, deployment manifests, SDKs (Python `iomete-sdk` and Java SDK under Apache-2.0), marketplace Spark jobs, templates and archived adapters; no platform service, console or Spark image source is public ([github.com/iomete](https://github.com/iomete)). "Open" therefore means open engines (Spark, Iceberg) and open formats, which is what underwrites the exit-path argument: standard Iceberg tables readable by "Any Iceberg-compatible engine" such as Trino, Flink, DuckDB and PyIceberg ([IOMETE: Databricks alternatives](https://iomete.com/resources/blog/databricks-alternatives)).

### Architecture: one Helm chart, about thirty services, Spark pods on demand

The documented architecture is three tiers (Kubernetes plus object storage; platform microservices in a single namespace; on-demand Spark workloads) deployed as one Helm chart, with compute and storage decoupled ([Architecture](https://iomete.com/resources/getting-started/architecture)). The services are an Nginx-based Gateway routing API, UI, Spark Connect, Arrow Flight, WebSocket and gRPC; Core (data-plane config, tokens, authorisation, node types); Cluster (compute clusters, Spark jobs, Jupyter containers, event streams); Identity (users, groups, roles, LDAP, SAML, OIDC, policy management); SQL (worksheets, Git, dashboards); Catalog (Typesense full-text search); an **Iceberg REST Catalog service in which "every request" is checked against Apache Ranger-style policies**; a Health Check service; Socket and Collaboration services (CRDT real-time SQL editing); NATS JetStream; a Prefect-based Job Orchestrator; the Spark Operator; and Spark History Server, all over one PostgreSQL server with separate databases for Hive Metastore, Ranger policies, the Iceberg catalog and each microservice ([Architecture](https://iomete.com/resources/getting-started/architecture)). Compute clusters are Spark driver pods exposing HiveServer2 on port 10000, Spark Connect on 15002 and Arrow Flight on 33333; Event Streams ingest HTTP POST JSON straight into Iceberg via a Rust service ([Architecture](https://iomete.com/resources/getting-started/architecture); [Deployment Architecture](https://iomete.com/resources/deployment/architecture-deployment)).

Versioning is fast and recent. The platform runs from v1.14.0 (June 13, 2024) through **v4.0.1 (October 8, 2026)**, with breaking majors at v2.0.0 (October 7, 2024: Keycloak and Ranger removed as separate components, integrated Iceberg REST Catalog) and **v4.0.0 (September 30, 2026: the platform "splits into one control plane and one or more data planes"**, SQL Editor V2 over Arrow Flight, Automated Table Maintenance in beta, a Comet "Query acceleration" toggle, encrypted DB secrets, runtime feature flags, and a Managed MCP Server in beta with 17 tools) ([Platform Release Notes](https://iomete.com/resources/deployment/on-prem/release-notes/)). Current images are Spark `3.5.7-v7` with Iceberg `1.9.0-iomete-5`, and a first Spark 4 image `4.1.3-v1` (Iceberg 1.11, format V3, DataFusion Comet 1.0.0) shipped October 1, 2026 with documented limits: no Nessie catalogs, column defaults rejected on Iceberg tables, and V3 tables unreadable by engines without V3 support ([Spark release notes](https://iomete.com/resources/deployment/on-prem/release-notes/spark); [Spark 4 Features](https://iomete.com/resources/reference/spark-4-features)). IOMETE 4.0's Comet integration cut TPC-DS 100GB total time **42% (28.6 to 16.5 minutes), with 99 of 103 queries faster**, but this is an IOMETE-vs-IOMETE comparison, not a competitor benchmark ([Comet post](https://iomete.com/resources/blog/apache-datafusion-comet-spark-acceleration)). Cadence is one to three releases a month.

### Deployment: any Kubernetes, Helm from chartmuseum, Terraform for Azure only

The deployment page lists on-premises on "any Kubernetes distribution" with "support for air-gapped environments," public cloud on EKS, AKS and GKE, private cloud on OpenShift and VMware Tanzu, and hybrid multi-cluster management ([Deployment](https://iomete.com/product/deployment)). A Nutanix Kubernetes Platform guide exists (Nutanix Objects, CloudNativePG, built-in Traefik rather than Istio), and the install guide requires a Kubernetes cluster, PostgreSQL, object storage (Apache Ozone, Dell ECS, IBM COS, MinIO, any S3-compatible store, S3, Azure Blob, GCS) and Istio ingress, pulls the chart from `https://chartmuseum.iomete.com`, and installs `iomete/iomete-data-plane-enterprise` into a namespace labelled `iomete.com/managed=true` ([Install](https://iomete.com/resources/deployment/on-prem/install)). Minimums are **12 CPU cores and 24 GB RAM for platform services plus one 4-CPU/32-GB node for Spark**; the sizing guide wants 1 CPU per 8 GB, local SSD for shuffle at 2 TB per 32 CPUs, and "the largest nodes your infrastructure supports" ([Install](https://iomete.com/resources/deployment/on-prem/install); [Sizing nodes](https://iomete.com/resources/user-guide/k8s/sizing-nodes-in-kubernetes-for-iomete-installation)). **Terraform "is currently available for Azure"** only ([Install](https://iomete.com/resources/deployment/on-prem/install)). Several things a buyer will ask are not published anywhere found: a supported Kubernetes version matrix, an OpenShift-specific guide, a dedicated air-gapped install procedure, the container registry hostname or image list for mirroring, and a maximum count of data planes, users or concurrent queries.

The feature surface is broad and almost entirely documented under `/resources/user-guide/`, `/resources/reference/` and `/resources/integrations/`: SAML 2.0, OIDC, Okta, Entra ID, OneLogin, SCIM and LDAP; row-level filters, seven column-masking types, tag-based policies and classifications with an approval workflow; audit via `platform_event_logs` and `data_access_audit` system tables; Iceberg time travel, procedures and maintenance; a dbt-iomete adapter (1.8.3, September 4, 2026) and an Airflow plugin (3.1.0, June 22, 2026); Power BI, Tableau (a `.taco` connector passing "all 926 TDVT tests"), Superset, Metabase and Redash; Hive JDBC and Arrow Flight SQL JDBC drivers; Python and Java SDKs; and an in-console REST API reference ([Integrations release notes](https://iomete.com/resources/deployment/on-prem/release-notes/integrations); [Single sign-on](https://iomete.com/resources/user-guide/single-sign-on); [Data Security overview](https://iomete.com/resources/user-guide/data-security/overview)). Two absences matter for honest selling: **there is no documented CLI**, and **data lineage is not shipped** (the roadmap lists "Data Lineage" as Backlog and the integrations page points to OpenMetadata and Collate), despite the homepage promising a catalog with "metadata, lineage, and quality information" ([iomete.com/roadmap](https://iomete.com/roadmap); [Integrations overview](https://iomete.com/resources/integrations/overview); [IOMETE homepage](https://iomete.com/)).

## A $500-per-vCPU licence with a $100,000 floor makes every deal a field sale

The live pricing page (fetched November 1, 2026) publishes three self-hosted tiers. **Free** is "The world's most generous free plan": zero licence cost, "Max 100 vCPUs," self-hosted on-premises, core features, community support. **Enterprise** is "$500 license cost per vCPU per year" with a footnoted "$100,000 per year (200 vCPU) minimum commitment," unlimited vCPUs, and "Core features + Data masking, Role level security, Disaster zone, Auditing" with Enterprise support. **Business Critical** is "Custom license cost" with a "$250,000 per year minimum commitment," on-premises or hybrid, "Enterprise features + Multi-region" and "Enterprise support + Dedicated engineers" ([IOMETE Pricing](https://iomete.com/pricing)). The term "Disaster zone" is defined nowhere. The Free plan is not a download: "Access is reviewed manually — tell us your real use case so we can provision it," with installation instructions delivered by email ([IOMETE Free Plan](https://iomete.com/pricing/free-plan)), so a Free-plan request is a top-of-funnel lead, not anonymous adoption. The 101–199 vCPU gap between the free cap and the Enterprise minimum is a structural objection that rfp.wiki paraphrases as "a high bar for mid-market buyers" ([RFP.wiki IOMETE profile](https://www.rfp.wiki/vendors/iomete)).

The model has changed three times, which matters because stale third-party pages still echo the old ones. In December 2023 the page promised "If you don't save > 50% over your existing solution we'll eat our shoe," priced on-premises at "$50 / per CPU core per month" and cloud by storage tier (free under 5 TB, $1,000/month for 5–50 TB, $5,000/month for 50–100 TB), and offered "Try Sandbox" and "Start Free Trial" CTAs that no longer exist ([Wayback 2 Dec 2023](https://web.archive.org/web/20231202055653/https://iomete.com/pricing)). In November 2024 public cloud was "$0.05 / per vCPU per hour" charged monthly and on-prem was quote-only ([Wayback 7 Nov 2024](https://web.archive.org/web/20241107201839/https://iomete.com/pricing)). The current three-tier model was in place by April 20, 2025 ([Wayback 20 Apr 2025](https://web.archive.org/web/20250420102031/https://iomete.com/pricing)). Arithmetic shows $500/vCPU/year is about $0.057/vCPU-hour at 8,760 hours, roughly revenue-neutral with the old hourly rate for always-on clusters but punitive for bursty ones. A pricing tracker lists "$500 per vCPU per month," which contradicts the page and IOMETE's own blog math and should be treated as an error ([PricingSaaS IOMETE](https://pricingsaas.com/companies/iomete)). Payment terms are "non-cancelable" and "non-refundable," set in an Order Form, with Delaware law and a liability cap of twelve months' fees (double for data-protection claims) ([Software License Agreement](https://iomete.com/policies/software-license-agreement)).

**No IOMETE listing exists on AWS, Azure or Google Cloud Marketplace** as far as searches and IOMETE's own silence show; a search snippet attributing "RU packs" and "managed Galaxy" to IOMETE is contamination from Starburst's pricing and is false ([RFP.wiki IOMETE vs Databricks](https://www.rfp.wiki/artificial-intelligence/data-lakehouse-platforms/iomete/databricks)). Procurement is direct or through a reseller under a separate "Reseller Arrangement" (SLA §6.4), and a three-track partner program (Technology, Implementation, Reseller) offers "competitive commissions on all referred business" without naming any partner in text ([IOMETE Partner Program](https://iomete.com/partner-program)). Visible partners are DataLead Consulting in Azerbaijan ([InfoCity, Oct 2024](https://infocity.tech/?p=194182)), and 2026 LinkedIn-announced channel deals with Albocensa (Romania), Dataxis Labs (India) and ICS Compute (Indonesia), plus claimed Nutanix Ready, SUSE Rancher, ThoughtSpot, Monte Carlo and Wasabi integrations that no partner page independently confirms ([LinkedIn: IOMETE](https://www.linkedin.com/company/iomete/)); the only third-party-confirmed integration is Ataccama ONE's documented IOMETE connection ([Ataccama docs](https://docs.ataccama.com/one/17.1.0/sources/iomete-connection.html)).

Support is response-time only, with no availability SLA because the customer runs the software. **Silver** ("Enterprise support") covers non-custom break/fix, five tickets a month, two portal users, Sev 1 response in one hour 24x7x365 and one business day for lower severities; **Gold** adds unlimited tickets, five users and Sev 2/3 at 4/8 hours; **Platinum** gives a dedicated engineer, Slack/email, and Sev 1 in 30 minutes ([IOMETE Pricing](https://iomete.com/pricing)). Gold's Sev 1 target was 30 minutes in the April and September 2025 captures and is one hour today, a quiet downgrade a tool should not misstate ([Wayback 20 Apr 2025](https://web.archive.org/web/20250420102031/https://iomete.com/pricing)). Support-tier prices, professional-services rates and the referenced "Support Policy" are not published; onboarding is "Scoped separately." The legal text disclaims the customer environment outright: "IOMETE is not responsible for the availability or performance of the Customer Environment" ([Software License Agreement](https://iomete.com/policies/software-license-agreement)).

The site funnel is uniform: every paid CTA is "Get in touch" or "Book a demo" through a form with a contact-reason dropdown ("Request a demo," "Technical support for existing customers," "Partnership opportunities," "Other") and no phone number or calendar embed ([IOMETE Contact](https://iomete.com/contact-us)), though the docs support page exposes a Calendly discovery call and a Jira Service Desk for existing customers ([Support](https://iomete.com/resources/getting-started/support)). The homepage de-positions "a startup with a few gigabytes" and aims at enterprises that "expect data sovereignty" at petabyte scale ([IOMETE homepage](https://iomete.com/)); Turkish press describes the go-to-market as "Direct sales to regulated sectors, with emphasis on data residency, control, and reduced dependence on external providers" ([Webrazzi, 11 Sep 2026](https://webrazzi.com/2026/09/11/kurumlarin-veriyi-kendi-altyapilarinda-tutmasina-odaklanan-girisim-iomete/)). IOMETE's own content gives the deal shape: first production workloads in **4–6 months, full Oracle migration in 18–36 months, steady state of 2–4 platform engineers** ([Data lakehouse for banks](https://iomete.com/resources/blog/data-lakehouse-for-banks)), and a break-even "typically 50-100TB and 50+ users" ([Fortune 500 TCO](https://iomete.com/resources/blog/fortune-500-cost-reduction-self-hosted-vs-saas-tco)).

For a business-case tool the cost stack is clear even where numbers are not: licence (vCPUs × $500, floored at $100K or $250K), support uplift (price unknown), one-time onboarding (unknown), compute at the buyer's post-discount cloud or on-prem rate, control-plane overhead, object storage, egress, platform-operations FTEs that IOMETE's tables omit, 3–12 months of migration engineering, and avoided or incurred FinOps tooling. IOMETE's own worked example prices 200 vCPUs at $100K licence plus 12.5 m5.4xlarge at $0.768/hour for 176 hours a month ($1,690) plus 50 TB of S3 at $0.023/GB ($1,150) for "about $134K/year," against a Snowflake range of $120K–$180K, which is inside the Snowflake range rather than 40–60% below it ([Fortune 500 TCO](https://iomete.com/resources/blog/fortune-500-cost-reduction-self-hosted-vs-saas-tco)). The same post's Profile 2 and 3 totals ($480K–$720K for 1,000 vCPUs; $1.44M–$3M for 3,000–5,000) are arithmetically below list licence alone, and a "100,000+ vCPUs" deployment described elsewhere would be $50M a year at list, so **list price demonstrably does not hold at scale**, and the tool should expose licence rate as an editable input rather than a constant ([Proof at Scale](https://iomete.com/resources/blog/sovereign-lakehouse-proof-at-scale)).

## Security is well documented at the data layer and silent at the corporate layer

The homepage shows "SOC2 Certified, HIPAA Compliant, GDPR Compliant" badges with no scope detail and links a Vanta Trust Center whose JavaScript-rendered contents could not be read programmatically ([IOMETE homepage](https://iomete.com/); [Trust Center](https://app.vanta.com/iomete.com/trust/mh7heslsoonlhblghsro7g)). The only certification evidence is a blog post: IOMETE "achieved SOC 2 Type I compliance" under SSAE 18 with an unqualified opinion, audited by Prescient Assurance, published December 25, 2022, with no Trust Services Criteria, audit period or report-request process stated ([SOC-2 blog](https://iomete.com/resources/blog/soc-achievement)). A removed Compliance & Finance Operations Lead posting (September 2026) assigned the hire to "Own and drive SOC 2 Type II compliance and audit readiness" and "Lead ISO 27001 readiness and certification preparation" ([Built In London listing](https://builtinlondon.uk/job/compliance-finance-operations-lead/9349489)), which is strong evidence that **Type II and ISO 27001 were not held as of mid-2026**. The FAQ answers "Can IOMETE meet GDPR, HIPAA, and SOC 2 requirements?" with "Yes, because you control the entire deployment," reframing vendor certification as customer-controlled compliance ([FAQ](https://iomete.com/faq)). PCI DSS, FedRAMP, CSA STAR and C5 are neither claimed nor found; `iomete.com/security`, `/policies/dpa` and `/policies/security-addendum` return 404 even though the licence agreement references a DPA and Security Addendum.

Where IOMETE is strong is exactly where a self-hosted vendor should be. The Security Overview documents local credentials, LDAP/AD sync, SAML/OIDC federation with Okta, OneLogin and Microsoft Entra ID, and SCIM provisioning; groups are "the primary unit of permission management" and can be nested; platform roles include `IAM_MANAGER` and `DATA_SECURITY_AND_AUDIT_MANAGER`; the Resource Authorization System grants per-resource permissions through bundles ([Security Overview](https://iomete.com/resources/user-guide/security-overview)). **MFA is not enforced by IOMETE** and is delegated to the identity provider; local-account MFA, password policy and session timeout are undocumented ([SSO Configuration](https://iomete.com/resources/user-guide/single-sign-on)). Masking offers Mask, Mask Hash, Mask Null, Show Last 4, Show First 4, Mask Date (Show Year), Custom SQL and None, applied "dynamically in the query output" ([Data Masking](https://iomete.com/resources/user-guide/data-security/data-masking)); row filters append WHERE conditions; classifications such as PII must be predefined and every change flows through Classification Requests ([Data Security Overview](https://iomete.com/resources/user-guide/data-security/overview)). Secrets live in Kubernetes Secrets or read-only HashiCorp Vault, with values "fetched at runtime" and never shown after save ([Secrets](https://iomete.com/resources/user-guide/secrets)); v4.0.0 encrypts platform secrets with **AES-256-GCM** using an install-time key and verifies control-plane-to-data-plane calls "with a shared trust secret" ([Release notes](https://iomete.com/resources/deployment/on-prem/release-notes/)). Three Kubernetes NetworkPolicies are documented, including a Jupyter policy whose egress blocks RFC1918 ranges ([Network Policies](https://iomete.com/resources/deployment/network-policies)). Audit is tabular: `platform_event_logs` "stores all platform audit events" and `data_access_audit` covers "all data access attempts, policy evaluations, and access control decisions," with a Ranger audit copy written as daily ORC files to object storage, but **retention, who may query, and SIEM export are not stated** ([System Tables](https://iomete.com/resources/user-guide/system-tables)). Release notes show steady CVE remediation (v3.18.0 patched "critical and high-severity CVEs in the Hive Metastore image" and closed "an unauthenticated WebSocket event-ingestion endpoint"; v4.0.1 fixed console JavaScript CVEs), and also one regression worth knowing: Spark image 3.5.7-v5 "reopens 11 Netty CVEs that 3.5.7-v4 had closed" ([Platform Release Notes](https://iomete.com/resources/deployment/on-prem/release-notes/); [Spark release notes](https://iomete.com/resources/deployment/on-prem/release-notes/spark)).

Encryption of data is delegated: at rest it is "handled by the storage layer using your own keys" with AWS KMS, Azure Key Vault, Google Cloud KMS and Vault integrations, and IOMETE "does not hold or manage your encryption keys itself" ([Regulated enterprises blog](https://iomete.com/resources/blog/data-lakehouse-for-regulated-enterprises)); in transit only ingress TLS (a Kubernetes TLS secret in `istio-system`) and optional PostgreSQL SSL are documented, with TLS versions, ciphers and service-to-service mTLS unaddressed ([Configure Ingress](https://iomete.com/resources/deployment/configure-ingress); [Deployment Architecture](https://iomete.com/resources/deployment/architecture-deployment)). Marketing phrases like "Military-grade encryption" and "End-to-end encryption" on the governance page have no documentation behind them ([Data governance](https://iomete.com/product/data-platform/data-governance)).

"Sovereign" and "air-gapped" are asserted, not procedurally documented. The FAQ says IOMETE "can be fully deployed and operated" in networks with no internet ([FAQ](https://iomete.com/faq)), and the sovereignty definition promises "no vendor data plane your data flows through" ([What is a sovereign data platform](https://iomete.com/resources/blog/what-is-a-sovereign-data-platform)), yet the install guide pulls the chart from chartmuseum.iomete.com and values files from GitHub, images moved to Azure Container Registry in v3.2.0 "to enable image immutability," the Private Docker Registry page names no default registry or image list, and licence activation, telemetry and offline updates are "Not covered" ([Install](https://iomete.com/resources/deployment/on-prem/install); [Private Docker Registry](https://iomete.com/resources/user-guide/k8s/private-docker-registry)). Crucially, **the licence agreement §4.2 reserves the right to collect "Usage Data" including "logs, telemetry, performance metrics, and metadata"** (excluding Customer Data) ([Software License Agreement](https://iomete.com/policies/software-license-agreement)), so a questionnaire helper must answer "telemetry: contractually permitted; mechanism and opt-out unknown" rather than "no phone-home." Vendor access is SOW-gated: IOMETE "will only access Customer Data when such access is specifically authorized in the applicable SOW," read-only and limited to dev/test unless stated (§10.4), with no breach-notification window, no audit rights and no subprocessor list in any public document ([Software License Agreement](https://iomete.com/policies/software-license-agreement)). No penetration-test summary, vulnerability disclosure policy, SECURITY.md, SBOM or image signing appears anywhere, including the GitHub organisation ([github.com/iomete](https://github.com/iomete)).

The answerability picture for a standard questionnaire follows directly. Against the CSA CAIQ v4.0's 17 domains and 261 questions (v4.1, released January 27, 2026, has 283) ([Wolfia CAIQ mapping](https://wolfia.com/blog/we-mapped-all-261-caiq-v4-questions-by-domain); [CSA CCM v4.1 transition](https://cloudsecurityalliance.org/blog/2026/02/19/ccm-v4-1-transition-timeline)), IAM, DSP, LOG, IVS and IPY are substantially answerable from public docs; CEK and AIS partially; A&A, GRC, HRS, CCC, SEF, STA, TVM and UEM are essentially unknown; and DCS and BCR are mostly "customer-controlled, N/A with explanation." Roughly six domains answerable, five partial, six unknown implies a public-source unknown rate in the **45–60% range**, which a tool must compute question by question rather than assert. Licensing constrains the denominator: CAIQ requires a free CSA account and a licence "to customize the CCM, use it commercially (including within products)" ([CSA Cloud Controls Matrix](https://cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/cloud-controls-matrix)); the SIG costs about $7,000/year and its 2025 agreement "broadly prohibits using licensed SIG content for AI training, testing, benchmarking, content generation" ([Vorp Labs SIG](https://vorplabs.com/security-framework-changes/sig)); the VSA-Core and VSA-Full are free ([UpGuard VSA](https://www.upguard.com/blog/vendor-security-alliance-questionnaire)); Google's VSAQ is Apache-2.0 but archived since November 25, 2022 ([google/vsaq](https://github.com/google/vsaq)). The pragmatic choice is VSA or IOMETE's own inbound questionnaires as the working set, with CAIQ domain names used for mapping.

### The objections arrive in a predictable order

From review-site risk statements and practitioner threads, objections to IOMETE run roughly: company viability and exit plan (sub-$3M disclosed funding, 11–50 staff, zero verified reviews, "Day-2 operations have limited independent third-party validation") ([RFP.wiki alternatives](https://www.rfp.wiki/artificial-intelligence/data-lakehouse-platforms/iomete/alternatives-and-competitors)); who runs Kubernetes and what day-2 costs are ("Self-hosted deployment shifts upgrades, capacity, and reliability work to the customer"); HA and zero-downtime upgrades (IOMETE documents replicas and rolling updates but `iom-sql` is a fixed single replica and backup/DR "are not covered") ([Scalability](https://iomete.com/resources/getting-started/scalability); [Deployment Architecture](https://iomete.com/resources/deployment/architecture-deployment)); missing Databricks conveniences, which IOMETE itself concedes ("Databricks leverages Photon engine optimizations," "curated GPU configurations and AutoML features") ([IOMETE: Databricks alternatives](https://iomete.com/resources/blog/databricks-alternatives)); scarce skilled staff and SI partners, versus Accenture's 5,500+ certified Databricks resources ([Addepto](https://addepto.com/blog/top-databricks-partners-for-implementation-migration-and-optimization/)); and the $100K minimum for pilots. Practitioners add the general case against self-hosting for small teams, "expensive compute is cheaper than expensive specialists" ([r/dataengineering snapshot](https://reddit.sentinel-team.org/posts/1qh4sp0/snapshots/2026-01-20T20%3A40%3A59.61641Z)). The honest counters are the Iceberg exit path, Spark skill transferability ("uses the same Apache Spark execution engine Databricks is built on"), the Dell deployment as the one external data point, Gartner Market Guide inclusion (which IOMETE itself notes "is not a ranking or a purchase recommendation") ([Gartner blog](https://iomete.com/resources/blog/iomete-gartner-market-guide-2025)), forward-deployed "Field Data Engineers" for operations ([Regulated enterprises blog](https://iomete.com/resources/blog/data-lakehouse-for-regulated-enterprises)), and the Dremio precedent that even mid-size independents lose neutrality.

## Enterprise data deals die in the paper process and on late ROI

The best-sourced benchmark of enterprise selling is Ebsta and Pavilion's 2024 analysis of 4.7 million opportunities and $57.3B of revenue across 566 companies: cycles ran **20% longer in H1 2024** than in 2023, win rates fell 7%, **44–46% of deals slipped** each quarter, and "majority of deals are being closed lost because a lack of momentum and lack of a decision maker, often caused by poor ICP fit, and poor multi-threading" ([Ebsta/Pavilion H1 2024](https://ebsta.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/H1-Update-2024-B2B-Sales-Benchmarks.pdf)). The same dataset shows **ROI and value objections up 127% since 2023, win rates down 64% when ROI is discussed late**, win rates up 14% when the senior finance persona is engaged by mid-stage, 3.1x higher win rates when opportunities match ICP (only 18% of pipeline did), and that top performers are 263% more likely to disqualify at discovery while 68% of deals pushed past qualification were not qualified. Top performers have a Director engaged in 88% of deals and a VP in 63–68% by the demo stage, so materials for three seniority levels are needed by mid-cycle. Vendor-authored lakehouse guides put data-platform deals at **6–12 months** ([rfp.wiki lakehouse RFP guide](https://www.rfp.wiki/artificial-intelligence/data-lakehouse-platforms)), and MEDDPICC guidance says the "paper process" of security review, legal redlines and procurement "commonly add 30 to 90 days to enterprise SaaS close dates when not mapped early," with security review typically running in parallel with the POC ([Altify](https://altify.com/blog/meddpicc-enterprise-sellers-qualification/)). Gartner's framing of buyer enablement adds the buyer-side mechanics: six "buying jobs" (problem identification, solution exploration, requirements building, supplier selection, validation, consensus creation) that groups must complete "with sufficient confidence" ([Gartner Buyer Enablement](https://gcom.pdo.aws.gartner.com/en/sales/insights/buyer-enablement)); buyers spend only about 17% of buying time with suppliers and are 3x more likely to make a high-quality purchase with buyer-enablement content ([Insightly citing Gartner](https://insightly.com/blog/buyer-enablement/)); and only 24% of rep-led purchases were "high-quality deals" versus 65% self-navigated ([Gartner press release, Jun 2022](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2022-06-21-gartner-says-marketing-leaders-must-reconceive-their-digital-experience)).

For IOMETE specifically this translates into three pressure points: a credible, early value story for finance (because the cost claims are contested and ROI objections are rising), a security review that is on the critical path from POC onward for every regulated buyer (and that IOMETE, with no public pen test or Type II, will answer slowly), and a champion who must carry the case to CISO, CDO and CFO with little social proof. At IOMETE's stage the people doing this are founders, one or two AEs, and engineers pulled into POCs and questionnaires; the founder-led-to-first-AE literature is consistent that an AE "can't build a playbook from scratch" ([Paraform](https://www.paraform.com/insights/hire-first-ae-technical-startup)), and Gartner finds 77% of sellers struggle to complete tasks efficiently despite rising tool budgets ([Gartner press release, Nov 2023](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2023-11-02-gartner-survey-finds-77-percent-of-sellers-struggle-to-complete-their-assigned-tasks-efficiently)), with tools failing when sellers "are unsure how to incorporate them effectively into their workflows, or don't want to take the extra steps required" ([Vivun citing Gartner](https://www.vivun.com/blog/why-sales-ai-isnt-working-for-you-and-how-to-fix-it)).

### What existing tools do well and badly, by friction

Call prep is a converged, crowded category. Attention's brief answers four questions (company overview with three trigger events, deal delta since last contact, attendee profiles, three opening questions) from CRM, transcripts, email, web, filings and LinkedIn, states "Manual pre-call research takes 30 to 60 minutes per account," and only since April 2026 cites web facts ([Attention blog](https://attention.com/blog-posts/ai-pre-call-account-research)); Momentum sells it at **$69–99/user/month** writing to Salesforce and Slack ([G2 Momentum pricing](https://g2.com/products/momentum-momentum/pricing)). The documented failure mode is fabrication: AI summaries "can misidentify champion status or fabricate confirmations of metrics," and one vendor traced **23% of late-stage losses** to a qualification element its AI had wrongly marked confirmed ([Spotlight.ai](https://www.spotlight.ai/post/ai-hallucinations-in-enterprise-sales-the-hidden-cost-your-cfo-has-not-calculated)); practitioners report tools that "require a supervisor" and two hours of verification per output ([Future of Prospecting](https://futureofprospecting.substack.com/p/account-research-tools-expectations)). Business-case tools are weaker than they look: Snowflake's calculator "cannot predict optimal warehouse sizing" and Databricks' "provides only an estimate" ([Snowflake calculator](https://snowflake.com/en/pricing-options/calculator/overview/); [Databricks calculator](https://databricks.com/product/gcp-pricing/instance-types)); lakehouse TCO studies are vendor-commissioned (GigaOm/Starburst "as much as 55% below" Snowflake; Dremio's 91%) ([Starburst](https://www.starburst.io/blog/snowflake-alternatives/); [Dremio deck](https://www.dremio.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/Snowflake-TCO-Deck.pdf)); the closest public model to IOMETE's architecture uses six categories (storage, compute, egress, engineering labour, operational overhead, lock-in cost) over three years with break-evens around 20–50 TB and ~20 engineers but omits Kubernetes self-hosting entirely ([IcebergLakehouse.com](https://iceberglakehouse.com/posts/open-lakehouse-vs-proprietary-warehouse-tco/)). Practitioner guidance on finance trust is unanimous: conservative customer-validated inputs, every input sourced, sensitivity cases, payback period over headline ROI, and "a hallucinated figure sent to a CFO damages credibility" ([Ecosystems](https://www.ecosystems.io/blog/how-to-build-a-business-case-for-enterprise-software); [Superintelligent Sales](https://superintelligentsales.beehiiv.com/p/salesgpt-roi-business-case-builder)). The 37signals repatriation story shows what finance attacks first, hardware refresh and ops headcount ([DHH FAQ](https://world.hey.com/dhh/the-big-cloud-exit-faq-20274010)).

Security questionnaire automation has consolidated (SafeBase into Drata; Vanta and Drata bundling trust centers). SafeBase shows the source document and last-modified date per answer and has an undocumented "high confidence" auto-approve, but **no flag for questions it cannot answer** ([SafeBase AIQA guide](https://help.safebase.io/en/articles/16203206-ai-questionnaire-assistance-aiqa-complete-guide)); Conveyor claims "every answer cited," an "AI Librarian" that flags gaps and conflicts, 95%+ first-pass accuracy, and ~50% for Loopio and Responsive, all vendor-reported ([Conveyor](https://conveyor.com/blog/loopio-vs-responsive-vs-conveyor)); pricing runs from Conveyor's $9,600/year to Loopio's ~$20,000 ([G2 Conveyor](https://g2.com/products/conveyor-conveyor/pricing); [ComplyJet](https://www.complyjet.com/blog/best-security-questionnaire-automation)). None models answers by deployment pattern (on-prem, VPC, air-gapped), and all report speed metrics rather than accuracy. Demo tooling splits into clones (Reprise $30K+, Demostack ~$2.6K/month), click-throughs (Navattic, Storylane, Walnut) and persona video (Consensus), none of which generate domain datasets, which is the real personalisation problem for a data platform ([HowdyGo](https://web.howdygo.com/blog/demostack-alternatives); [Prospeo](https://prospeo.io/s/demostack-alternatives)). Buyer enablement has settled on digital sales rooms with mutual action plans; Trumpet's uncontrolled platform data claims MAPs double win rates and rooms with 10+ stakeholders close at 75% ([Trumpet](https://www.sendtrumpet.com/blog-posts/do-digital-sales-rooms-actually-work)).

The convergent "trust spec" across these sources is five properties: every output traces to a source; gaps are acknowledged instead of filled; each conclusion has defined evidence requirements; thin evidence suppresses output; and humans review with an audit trail ([Spotlight.ai](https://www.spotlight.ai/post/ai-hallucinations-in-enterprise-sales-the-hidden-cost-your-cfo-has-not-calculated)). Salesforce's Einstein Trust Layer implements permission-aware "dynamic grounding" ([Trailhead](https://trailhead.salesforce.com/zh-CN/content/learn/modules/the-einstein-trust-layer/meet-the-einstein-trust-layer)). Because IOMETE has almost no reviews, sourcing and assumption labelling are not polish; they are the substitute for social proof.

## Scoring the six frictions: build the questionnaire helper and the business-case builder

The scores below apply the track's weights (practical impact 35%, buyer clarity 25%, accuracy 25%, working execution 15%) on a 1–10 scale, grounded in the evidence above rather than in enthusiasm. "Accuracy" here means the achievable rate of fabrication-free output given the public corpus; "execution" means the odds of a working, demonstrable build without proprietary data or platform access.

| Candidate | Impact (35%) | Clarity (25%) | Accuracy (25%) | Execution (15%) | Weighted | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Security questionnaire helper | 9 | 7 | 9 | 7 | **8.2** | Build first |
| Business case builder | 9 | 8 | 8 | 7 | **8.2** | Build second, same evidence store |
| Role-based explainer | 6 | 8 | 6 | 8 | 6.8 | Fold into the two above as output modes |
| Use-case fit quiz | 6 | 7 | 7 | 8 | 6.8 | Cheap qualifier; low marginal value |
| Demo personalizer | 5 | 6 | 6 | 3 | 5.2 | Do not build without platform access |
| 15-minute call prep | 5 | 3 | 4 | 4 | 4.1 | Do not build; buy |

The **security questionnaire helper** earns its impact score because the paper process is a documented 30–90 day drag, security review is on the POC critical path for regulated buyers, IOMETE's buyers are by definition regulated, and the vendor has no dedicated security-response function at 11–50 people, so every questionnaire consumes engineer time. It earns its accuracy score because this research already produced the raw material: a DOCUMENTED / IMPLIED / UNKNOWN matrix across roughly thirty topics with exact URLs, and a self-hosted answer pattern that generic tools lack. The self-hosted pattern is the design centre: hosting region, data-center controls, backups, encryption-at-rest keys, network perimeter and DR answer "Customer-controlled: IOMETE deploys into the customer's Kubernetes cluster and object storage; IOMETE does not host customer data," marked N/A-with-explanation rather than unknown; while telemetry (SLA §4.2), support access (§10.4), supply chain (registry, signing, SBOM), the vendor's corporate program (Type I 2022 only), breach notification and subprocessors must never be marked N/A and must route to a named owner when unknown ([Software License Agreement](https://iomete.com/policies/software-license-agreement)). Clarity is 7 rather than 9 because the audience is a buyer's security team and procurement, not the whole buying group. Execution is 7 because the public corpus is enough for a first pass but the Vanta Trust Center must be read manually and the SIG cannot be used.

The **business-case builder** ties on the weighted score for the opposite reasons: finance is the persona whose early engagement most moves win rates, ROI objections are rising fastest, and IOMETE's own cost story is the single most contradictory thing it publishes. A tool that models the ten-line cost stack, treats licence rate, ops FTEs, hardware refresh and utilisation as editable ranged inputs, labels every number as customer-provided, public list price or IOMETE assumption, and leads with sensitivity and payback rather than a headline percentage would be the first honest instrument in the category. It must never emit "2–5x," "40–60%" or "more than 2x" as results; it may quote them as IOMETE claims with URLs. The cost of that honesty is political: on a well-negotiated, high-utilisation Databricks estate the tool will show roughly what IOMETE's own e-commerce example showed (about 1%), and the sovereignty argument must carry the deal. That is a feature, since the Ebsta data says late ROI surprises are what kill deals, and IOMETE's content already positions regulation as the lead argument where cost is thin.

The **role-based explainer** is clear and easy but low in marginal impact because IOMETE's 2026 blog already supplies CFO ("CFOs hate consumption-based pricing because it's unpredictable"), CISO ("trust perimeter," "sovereign by architecture") and CTO ("Kubernetes by choice, not by capability") framings ([Fortune 500 TCO](https://iomete.com/resources/blog/fortune-500-cost-reduction-self-hosted-vs-saas-tco); [Regulated enterprises blog](https://iomete.com/resources/blog/data-lakehouse-for-regulated-enterprises)), and because generating persona prose is where a model is most tempted to repeat the contradictory savings figures. Its best form is an output mode of the two recommended tools: the questionnaire helper emits a CISO summary, the business case emits a CFO summary, and both emit a one-page architect view. The **use-case fit quiz** could honestly encode the documented thresholds (100-vCPU free cap, 200-vCPU minimum, Kubernetes prerequisite, 12-CPU/24-GB floor, air-gap and regulatory drivers, de-positioning of gigabyte-scale startups) and would support early disqualification, but the Free-plan form already asks for the use case and reviews it manually, so a quiz adds little. The **demo personalizer** fails on execution: the Free plan is manually gated, there is no sandbox since 2023, and the real work is seeded, industry-plausible Iceberg datasets and narrative scripts that need product access and SE time. The **call-prep generator** fails on three of four axes: commercial tools do it for $69–99/user/month, IOMETE's deal volume is small, the key inputs (CRM, transcripts, LinkedIn) are unavailable to a hackathon team and were unreachable even to this research, and the fabrication risk is the category's documented weakness.

### Data sources and authority ranking for the build

The shared evidence store should ingest, in descending authority: the platform, Spark, marketplace-jobs and integrations release notes at `/resources/deployment/on-prem/release-notes/`; the deployment and user-guide pages (install, architecture-deployment, network-policies, configure-ingress, secrets, system-tables, security-overview, data-security/*, single-sign-on/*); the legal pages (software-license-agreement, privacy-policy) and the pricing pages; the FAQ and llms.txt as secondary; the homepage, product pages and blog as attributed marketing claims; the roadmap for status; the GitHub organisation for repo facts; and the Vanta Trust Center via manual capture. Every record should carry the URL, a fetch date, a quoted span, a source-tier tag and a state (documented, inferred, unknown). The iom-docs repo can be cloned and mapped to URLs mechanically; the Algolia index can serve lookups; `llms-full.txt` should be checked. Third-party aggregators (rfp.wiki, extruct, Owler, PricingSaaS) should be stored only as "claims about IOMETE" and never surfaced as facts, given the demonstrated Starburst contamination. The customer section should be stored as tiers (described deployment, logo, anonymised, partner claim) and rendered with the tier visible.

### What the tools must refuse to assert

Beyond the contradictions table, the refusal list is: that any logo is a customer; that the Dell 20 PB figure or the 100,000-vCPU deployment is independently verified; any G2, Capterra or Peer Insights rating; a headcount or funding total more precise than "11–50" and "under $3M disclosed"; SOC 2 Type II, ISO 27001, PCI DSS, FedRAMP or CSA STAR; the scope or criteria of the 2022 Type I; a HIPAA BAA or published DPA; that there is no telemetry or phone-home; a documented air-gapped install, image list or offline update path; image signing, SBOM, pen testing or a disclosure policy; a breach-notification window; audit-log retention or SIEM export; service-to-service mTLS; backup and DR procedures; shipped data lineage; a CLI; Pulsar, Looker or Qlik connectors; a Kubernetes version matrix; an uptime SLA; support-tier prices, volume discounts or a Business Critical rate card; the meaning of "Disaster zone"; a cloud-marketplace listing; "no egress" (IOMETE budgets egress at about $0.02/GB); any savings percentage as an outcome rather than an attributed claim; and any `docs.iomete.com` URL.

### How to prove it works

For the questionnaire helper, run a free questionnaire (VSA-Core or a CAIQ domain subset used internally) end to end and publish per-domain counts of documented, N/A-with-explanation, inferred and unknown answers; the honest unknown rate from public sources will land somewhere near 45–60%, and that number is the demo, not an embarrassment. Then run a zero-fabrication test: a fixed set of questions whose true public answer is unknown (ISO 27001, pen test cadence, SBOM, breach notification window, telemetry endpoints, audit retention) must return UNKNOWN with an owner routing, every time, across repeated runs; a single confident wrong answer fails the test. Have an IOMETE engineer grade a random 30 answers against the cited URL, log the edit rate after review as SafeBase implicitly does, and show that approved edits flow back into the store with a new freshness date. For the business-case builder, reproduce IOMETE's own 200-vCPU worked example to the dollar from its stated inputs ($100K licence, 12.5 m5.4xlarge at $0.768/hour for 176 hours, 50 TB at $0.023/GB, about $134K/year), then show the Profile 1 inconsistency the post itself contains, then demonstrate that a Databricks-heavy high-utilisation scenario yields a near-zero saving, which proves the model is not rigged. Hand the output to someone playing a sceptical finance reviewer with instructions to attack hardware refresh and ops headcount; the tool passes if both are exposed as ranged inputs with provenance. For both, time-to-first-draft is a secondary metric; cited-fact rate and fabrication rate are primary, because speed is what every commercial tool already measures and accuracy is what none of them publish.

## Conclusion

The deepest finding is that IOMETE's documentation is more trustworthy than its marketing, and a tool that chooses the documentation as its source of truth will sometimes contradict the company's own homepage. That is not a defect to be engineered around; it is the product. A vendor with no verified reviews, no named case studies and a single four-year-old Type I report cannot win regulated enterprise deals on social proof, so the only currency it has is verifiable specificity: exact URLs, exact version numbers, exact clauses in its own licence, and an explicit "we do not know" where the evidence stops. The two recommended tools are the two places where that currency is spent at the moment it matters most, when a CISO's team opens a questionnaire and when a CFO asks for the number, and both can be built on the same evidence store with the same three-state epistemics.

The second implication is organisational rather than technical. Every "unknown" the questionnaire helper surfaces is a backlog item for IOMETE's compliance lead, and every "IOMETE assumption" the business-case builder exposes is a number the company should either defend or retire. Run honestly, the tools become an audit of the company's public evidence base and a roadmap for closing it: publish the air-gap procedure and image list, finish the Type II, define "Disaster zone," retire the 2–5x claim or source it, and caption the logo wall. A planning-phase product team should budget for that feedback loop, because a sales-enablement tool that only reflects the gaps without a path to close them will be switched off by the first rep who is embarrassed by it.
